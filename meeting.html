Where Brazilian differs from American

Not a primer. The risk isn't skill, it's defaulting to US patterns under pressure — and several of them are read here as statements about character, not just style.

🔴 The phrases that mean no

A direct não is rare. Disinterest arrives as warmth. These usually mean no:

  • "Vou ver e te aviso" — I'll check and let you know
  • "Depois a gente combina" — we'll sort it out later
  • "Vamos marcar" — let's set something up
  • "Pode ser!" — yeah, we could do that
  • "Vamos ver" / "talvez" — let's see / maybe
  • "Tá, a gente se fala" — ok, we'll talk
The discriminator is specificity, not warmth

Enthusiasm is the baseline register of ordinary politeness, so warmth proves nothing. A concrete next step she proposes — a date, a name, a question about mechanics, an introduction — is the real signal. Warmth is free. Specificity costs her something. Leave with what she committed to, not how it felt.

🔴 Urgency and scarcity are the worst move

  • Time pressure and expiring offers are read as "signs that you are not willing to build a long-term relationship." Attempts to rush "may be viewed as offensive."
  • The damage isn't annoyance — the tactic tells her what kind of counterparty you are.
  • Same family: assumptive close, "what would it take to earn your business," aggressive follow-up cadence. Persistence reads as commitment in the US; here the trust signal is patience.

🟡 Don't time-box, and don't say you're respecting her time

  • Leaping into business is considered crass. Meetings open with extensive small talk and run polychronically — jumping between topics, not down an agenda.
  • "I'll be respectful of your time" is a US virtue that here reads as "I have priced this interaction."
  • Decisions are rarely made in a first meeting. Expect not to close — that's normal, not failure.
  • Small talk range is wide, but private life is not an early subject. Her work, the move, the city, Rafa — yes. Anything personal — let her go first.

🟡 Start formal and let her release you

  • Brazil scores 69 on power distance vs 40 for the US. Status, rank and education are emphasised.
  • Open with Senhora + surname, or a title with the first name. Wait to be invited to first names.
  • Peer-casual informality is a US status-leveling move that misfires here. Offering formality is the respect move, and her releasing you is the relationship progressing.
  • Greeting: firm handshake, eye contact, warm smile. Cheek kisses are common between women even in business — let her initiate, match, don't lead.

🟡 Let her raise price

  • General Brazil is a hard-bargaining culture — prices can move 40%+ from first offer, and a firm take-it-or-leave-it number can insult.
  • But she teaches pricing to other consultants. Her professional identity is built on holding a price. Weight that over the country generalisation.
  • Resolution: flexibility of scope, not of number. Tiers and phasing satisfy both. Highest tier first.

🟢 What the referral actually buys — and obligates

  • Business runs on personal connection. "Sometimes all it takes is mentioning the name of a mutual friend." Rafa's referral has already granted standing that would take several meetings to earn.
  • Trust attaches to a person, not a company — it doesn't transfer to others at the same firm.
  • ⚠️ It also makes refusing you harder, which makes misreading politeness more likely, not less. And any pressure spends Rafa's credibility, not just yours. Treat it as a reason to be less pushy, not permission to move faster.
  • No gift at a first meeting — it "raises suspicions about your motives."

🟢 Jeitinho — rigidity is the failure mode

  • Brazilians "cannot separate public and private dimensions" — a businessperson is the citizen and the company at once.
  • "That's our standard process" / "the package is fixed" each read as a small refusal of relationship.
  • Willingness to shape something around her specifically is a trust signal — and it's available without touching price.
Caveat worth holding

Most sources describe doing business in Brazil, usually corporate, usually men. She lives in Florida and sells in a US market, so treat this as the right prior, not a certainty. The anti-pressure finding is the one to hold regardless — being wrong about it is cheap, being right is decisive.
